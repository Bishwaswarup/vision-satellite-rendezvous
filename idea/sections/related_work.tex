\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

This section reviews the bodies of work the present study draws on ---
autonomous rendezvous, monocular relative pose estimation, robust
correspondence estimation, and constrained guidance and control --- and then
states, in Section~\ref{sec:gap}, the question their intersection leaves open.

\subsection{Autonomous Rendezvous and Proximity Operations}
\label{sec:rw_arpo}

Automated rendezvous and docking underpins on-orbit servicing, debris removal
and in-space assembly \cite{fehse2003rendezvous,flores2014}; its translational
dynamics are conventionally described by the Hill--Clohessy--Wiltshire (HCW)
linearisation \cite{clohessy1960}. The current generation of missions differs
in that the target is \emph{non-cooperative}: no docking aids, no
retro-reflectors, no telemetry link, and in general tumbling. RemoveDEBRIS
exercised a vision-based navigation chain against an uncontrolled target
during net and harpoon capture \cite{aglietti2020removedebris}, and AVANTI
achieved autonomous mid-range rendezvous with a non-cooperative object from
angles-only measurements \cite{gaias2018avanti}. Both show the guidance layer
to be mature, and the binding constraint to have moved to pose estimates of
adequate quality \emph{and adequate frequency} from a passive camera.

\subsection{Model-Based Monocular Relative Pose Estimation}
\label{sec:rw_pose}

Recovering the six-degree-of-freedom relative pose of a known target from a
single image is normally cast as a Perspective-$n$-Point (PnP) problem, shown
viable for rendezvous and docking by Kelsey~\textit{et al.}~\cite{kelsey2006}
and surveyed across monocular, stereo and LIDAR architectures
\cite{opromolla2017review,cassinis2019review}.

The EPnP algorithm \cite{lepetit2009epnp} remains widely used onboard for its
$\mathcal{O}(n)$ complexity, writing the reference points as a weighted
combination of four virtual control points and recovering the pose from a
compact eigen-decomposition. Two of its properties, treated in
Section~\ref{sec:epnp_gn}, matter here: the four-control-point construction
degenerates for coplanar reference points, which Lepetit~\textit{et al.}
handle by dropping to three; and accuracy without the $\beta$ refinement of
the null-space coefficients is materially worse.

Ke and Roumeliotis~\cite{ke2017ap3p} give a direct algebraic P3P solution
avoiding the depth polynomial, and Terzakis and
Lourakis~\cite{terzakis2020sqpnp} present SQPnP, globally optimal for the
least-squares PnP cost and stable in planar and quasi-degenerate
configurations. The framework here nonetheless retains EPnP, since the
question asked --- when is a pose solution \emph{available} --- is largely
independent of the non-iterative solver; Section~\ref{sec:res_pose} quantifies
the accuracy cost.

\subsection{Learning-Based Pose Estimation and Its Benchmarks}
\label{sec:rw_learned}

Since 2018, learned front ends have largely displaced hand-designed feature
extraction. Sharma~\textit{et al.}~\cite{sharma2018} first applied a
convolutional network to non-cooperative spacecraft pose estimation; Sharma
and D'Amico~\cite{sharma2020nn} developed it into the Spacecraft Pose Network;
and Proen{\c{c}}a and Gao~\cite{proenca2020urso} trained a pose regressor on
photorealistically rendered scenes, releasing the URSO datasets. The Satellite
Pose Estimation Challenge established the SPEED dataset and a common metric
\cite{kisantal2020}; SPEED+ \cite{park2022speedplus} added
hardware-in-the-loop and lightbox imagery exposing the synthetic-to-real
domain gap, with the follow-on competition analysed by Park~\textit{et
al.}~\cite{park2023spec2021}; and later work attacks that gap through
multi-task learning and online refinement \cite{park2024spnv2} and
flight-ready architectures trained only on synthetic renderings
\cite{park2026spnv3}. Reported accuracies are now a few tenths of a degree in
orientation and a fraction of a percent of range in position.

Three features bear on the present paper. First, the benchmarks are
\emph{open-loop}: the camera trajectory that produced the scored images was
not generated by a controller acting on the network's own output. Second, the
metrics are conditioned on a solution being produced: images on which
detection fails are excluded or absorbed into an aggregate score, so the
benchmark reports the accuracy of the estimates that exist rather than the
rate at which they exist. Third, learned front ends need labelled data and
give no geometric guarantee, which is why model-based pipelines remain the
reference point for known geometry; the present work follows that route,
treating its front end as a source of \emph{intermittent} measurements.

\subsection{Robust Correspondence Estimation}
\label{sec:rw_ransac}

Wrong correspondences degrade pose estimation sharply, and under occlusion,
specular glint, Earth-limb background or repeated target structure they are
the normal case. Random Sample Consensus \cite{fischler1981ransac} remains
standard: minimal-sample hypotheses are scored by consensus-set size, with the
iteration budget adapted online from the running inlier ratio. LO-RANSAC
\cite{chum2003loransac} refits the model on each so-far-best hypothesis's full
inlier set; USAC \cite{raguram2013usac} unifies sampling, verification,
degeneracy detection and local refinement, with largely complementary
component gains; and MAGSAC++ \cite{barath2020magsacpp} removes the
inlier--outlier threshold by marginalising over the noise scale under an
iteratively reweighted least-squares optimisation. The framework here uses
adaptive-termination RANSAC with EPnP as hypothesis solver and a final inlier
refit on the $SO(3)$ manifold; the breakdown point of
Section~\ref{sec:res_ransac} is what a threshold-free estimator would improve.

\subsection{Recursive State Estimation for Tumbling Targets}
\label{sec:rw_filter}

Kalman-type estimators fuse noisy pose measurements with a relative-motion
model. Because an additive extended Kalman filter cannot preserve the
unit-quaternion norm through the update, the standard remedy is the
multiplicative EKF (MEKF), propagating a small rotation-vector error state
composed multiplicatively with a reference quaternion \cite{markley2003}; the
unscented Kalman filter \cite{julier2004} instead propagates sigma points and
needs no Jacobians, at the cost of more dynamics evaluations. Both are
compared here (Section~\ref{sec:res_filter}), and, as
Section~\ref{sec:mekf_residual} sets out, the attitude residual is formed
multiplicatively on the group rather than by differencing global rotation
vectors --- a distinction invisible for small errors and dominant near the
$\pi$-radian singularity a tumbling target visits routinely.

The closest work on coupling is that of Pasqualetto Cassinis~\textit{et
al.}~\cite{cassinis2021coupled}, who compare tightly- and loosely-coupled
arrangements of a CNN detector, a covariance-aware PnP solver and an MEKF, and
show that propagating the image-level covariance into the filter couples
rotational and translational states advantageously. That couples the
\emph{uncertainty} of the pose solution to the filter, not its
\emph{existence} to a controller, because there is no controller in the loop:
the trajectory is prescribed.

\subsection{Constrained Guidance and Control for Proximity Operations}
\label{sec:rw_control}

Linear quadratic regulation gives a closed-form infinite-horizon feedback law
from the discrete algebraic Riccati equation \cite{anderson1990}, but cannot
express the constraints that define a safe approach: a thrust bound, a
keep-out zone, an approach corridor. Model predictive control supplies exactly
that by re-solving a finite-horizon constrained program at every step
\cite{mayne2000}. Di Cairano~\textit{et al.}~\cite{dicairano2012mpc}
formulated proximity manoeuvring with thrust bounds and line-of-sight
constraints, showing that for a stationary platform an explicit offline
solution removes onboard optimisation entirely; Breger and
How~\cite{breger2008} generated passively safe trajectories under thruster
failure; and Specht~\textit{et al.}~\cite{specht2023tubempc} applied
tube-based MPC to rendezvous, guaranteeing constraint satisfaction under
bounded disturbance.

The present work does not improve on these formulations. It implements a
receding-horizon controller with a second-order approach-cone constraint and
soft slack for recursive feasibility (Section~\ref{sec:cone}) and evaluates it
against LQR on the same scenario, keeping the guidance layer representative
while the variable of interest remains perception. One point is, however,
load-bearing: every formulation above assumes the state estimate is
\emph{available at the control rate}. A tube bounds a disturbance, not a
measurement outage; an explicit map is a function of a state assumed known.
Intermittent measurement is outside the disturbance model these guarantees are
built on.

\subsection{Closed-Loop Simulation Frameworks}
\label{sec:rw_sim}

Gasbarri~\textit{et al.}~\cite{testbed2014} present a ground-based facility
for vision-based formation flying, and the hardware-in-the-loop imagery behind
SPEED+ and its successors \cite{park2022speedplus,park2026spnv3} comes from
testbeds of the same kind. Such facilities reproduce the photometric realism
synthetic rendering struggles with, but not closed-loop causality: the robot
follows a commanded trajectory, so the images the estimator sees do not depend
on the estimates the controller acted on. The present work makes the opposite
trade, forgoing photometric realism for a closed causal loop in which the
viewpoint at step $k+1$ follows from the pose solution at step $k$, so a lost
measurement propagates.

\subsection{Gap Addressed by This Work}
\label{sec:gap}

Taken together, this literature characterises the two halves of a vision-based
rendezvous system very well, and characterises them separately.

On one side, the pose-estimation literature reports \emph{accuracy}: rotation
and translation error against range, illumination, architecture and domain, on
fixed image sets
\cite{kisantal2020,park2023spec2021,park2024spnv2,park2026spnv3,cassinis2019review}.
These numbers are conditioned on a pose being returned. On the other, the
guidance and control literature reports \emph{closed-loop outcomes}:
propellant, terminal accuracy, constraint satisfaction, and --- in Monte Carlo
studies --- docking success rate, assuming the state estimate arrives at the
control rate \cite{dicairano2012mpc,breger2008,specht2023tubempc}. Work that
bridges the two, such as \cite{cassinis2021coupled}, does so at the level of
\emph{covariance}: it asks how measurement uncertainty should enter the
filter, not how often a measurement enters at all.

The consequence is that a designer holding both halves can predict neither of
the two things they most need. Given a measured pose accuracy, no model says
whether the closed loop will close; given a closed-loop success rate, there is
no attribution of that number to the perception chain, because the two are not
connected by any statement about measurement \emph{availability}. Availability
is not a free parameter of the perception system: it is a geometric
consequence of the same approach trajectory the controller is generating. As
the chaser closes, the target's projected extent grows as $1/z$ until it
exceeds the field of view, at which point the visible keypoint set collapses
--- and it collapses precisely in the terminal phase, where the control
authority needed to recover is smallest.

This paper closes that gap. Its contributions are, first, a closed-loop
simulation in which perception, estimation and control are causally coupled,
so that measurement dropout is generated by the trajectory rather than imposed
on it; second, a closed-form criterion for the range at which field-of-view
saturation begins to remove keypoints, $z_{\text{fill}} = fL/W$
(Section~\ref{sec:res_dropout}), validated against the measured dropout knee;
and third, a dispersed Monte Carlo characterisation
(Section~\ref{sec:res_configs}) showing that closed-loop performance is
insensitive to pose accuracy across almost an order of magnitude of pixel
noise and then fails abruptly, with the failure attributable to availability
rather than accuracy. The practical implication --- that the design margin
worth buying is field of view and keypoint redundancy at short range, not
sub-pixel feature localisation --- is not derivable from either half of the
literature alone.
