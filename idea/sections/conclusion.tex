\section{Conclusion}

This paper presented a modular closed-loop simulation framework for
vision-based rendezvous with a tumbling non-cooperative target, and asked what the pose-estimation and guidance literatures do not answer
between them: given a monocular front end of known accuracy, will the loop
close?

Each subsystem was validated in isolation. The analytical HCW
state-transition implementation agrees with DOP853 integration to
$4.55\times10^{-10}$~m over three orbital periods. EPnP with full $\beta$
refinement matches a reference implementation to within a factor of 1.2 in
rotation error and solves exactly coplanar keypoint subsets --- approximately
3\,\% of random six-point samples --- that the reference rejects. RANSAC--EPnP
fails in no trial up to 30\,\% outlier contamination; bare EPnP fails in
99\,\% of trials at 5\,\%. The MEKF and UKF are indistinguishable in
translation, as exactly linear HCW dynamics require, and differ only in
attitude, where the MEKF is marginally better and faster.

The closed-loop result is that the binding constraint is measurement
\emph{availability}, set by geometry rather than noise. Pooled over 3887
control steps of 100 dispersed Monte Carlo trials, the pose solve succeeds on
100\,\% of steps beyond 6~m and on 1.8\,\% inside 1~m. The transition is
predictable in closed form: a target of characteristic length $L$ fills a sensor
of width $W$ pixels at $z_{fill} = fL/W$, here 6.25~m, and the first range bin
whose availability interval excludes unity is 5--6~m. Inside $z_{fill}$ the
keypoints simultaneously in frame fall below the six a minimal RANSAC sample
requires, and no better solver recovers them.

Pose RMSE is therefore the wrong figure of merit here. In a paired ablation
over 40 dispersed trials per level, the median terminal range is flat from 0.3
to 3.0~px, varying by 4~cm while keypoint noise grows tenfold and the pose error
with it, and the degradation that follows is a threshold rather than a drift:
the median trial at 4~px docks as tightly as the median trial at 0.3~px, while
the 95th percentile of terminal range rises from 0.73~m to 12.47~m and docking
success falls from 100\,\% to 85\,\% [71,\,93]. Roughly one trial in seven
diverges completely, because the solver's success rate inside $z_{fill}$ falls
low enough that the filter coasts on prediction through the terminal phase.
Terminal approach inside the fill range thus requires a change of sensing --- a
wider lens, a second short-range camera, or a keypoint subset chosen to stay in
frame --- and that, not sub-pixel feature localisation, is the margin worth
buying.

Two methodological points support these conclusions. The campaign disperses
initial range, bearing, closing speed, tumble rate, target attitude and
navigation initialisation error, not only the seed; along a single fixed
approach the same ablation collapses far more sharply, because every trial then
meets the field-of-view knee at identical geometry and tumble phase.
Availability must also be measured against the range the camera is at, not the
range a trial began from: binned by initial range the dropout rate is
34.3\,\%, 33.3\,\% and 31.7\,\%, carrying no information because every trial
that closes traverses the whole interval.

The principal limitation is the front end. Correspondences are known and their
projection analytic, so the perception chain is idealised: real imagery adds
illumination variation, specular glint, Earth-limb background, partial
visibility and matching ambiguity, all of which would reduce availability
further and none of which would move $z_{fill}$, set by the optics and target
alone. The availability result is therefore best read as an upper bound.
Future work should re-measure the availability profile against a rendered or
hardware-in-the-loop front end; extend the analysis to a threshold-free robust
estimator, whose principal benefit would be to the breakdown point measured
here; and address the terminal phase directly, through the multi-sensor
arrangement the fill criterion implies or a guidance law that anticipates the
outage rather than coasting through it.
