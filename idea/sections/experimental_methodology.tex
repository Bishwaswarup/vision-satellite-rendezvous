\section{Experimental Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

The architecture is examined in six controlled experiments. Each subsystem is
validated against an independent reference before the complete
vision--estimation--control loop is exercised, so that numerical and
algorithmic errors can be separated from behaviour that emerges only when the
components interact. The experiments address three escalating questions: that
the constituent models hold, that the individual algorithms tolerate
perturbations, and that the assembled architecture still functions when they
are coupled.

\subsection{Simulation Environment and Common Parameters}

The testbed is implemented in Python. Relative translational motion is
propagated in the LVLH frame with the HCW equations and target attitude with
the rigid-body model of Section~III, using adaptive high-order integration
(DOP853) where numerical propagation is required. The target is a parametric
Ariane 44L upper stage with fifteen analytically defined body-frame landmarks,
projected through the calibrated pinhole camera of Section~III-D. Unless an
experiment states otherwise, the parameters of
Table~\ref{tab:experimental_parameters} apply, and every trial is driven by an
explicitly recorded seed so that it is reproducible in isolation.

\subsection{Experimental Campaign}

\paragraph{A --- Dynamics validation}
The closed-form HCW state-transition solution is compared against DOP853
numerical propagation of a periodic trajectory with an initial radial
amplitude of 100 m over three orbital periods. The maximum position and
velocity discrepancies over the interval are the validation metrics, and the
integration tolerance is then swept to confirm that these discrepancies are
converging rather than merely small.

\paragraph{B --- Pixel-noise sensitivity}
The image-plane noise level is varied and the raw EPnP pose compared with the
pose after nonlinear refinement, at a fixed range and target attitude so that
the geometry contributes nothing to the trend. This isolates the accuracy the
front end can deliver from the accuracy it retains once the refinement stage
is removed, and quantifies what that stage is worth.

\paragraph{C --- Outlier robustness}
A controlled fraction of the keypoints is replaced by image coordinates drawn
uniformly over the frame, leaving the inlier noise fixed. Bare EPnP is
compared with RANSAC--EPnP. A trial counts as failed when the solver reports
an invalid solution or the recovered rotation error exceeds the threshold in
Table~\ref{tab:experimental_parameters}; errors are reported over successful
trials and the failure rate over all trials, so that failed solves are not
silently removed from the error statistics.

\paragraph{D --- State-estimation comparison}
The MEKF and UKF receive an identical sequence of direct pose measurements
generated from the true state with additive Gaussian noise, rather than
measurements produced by the camera and PnP stages. The comparison therefore
characterises the filtering formulation under a known measurement
distribution, and is not an end-to-end camera-navigation result. Tracking
error, gating behaviour, innovation consistency and wall-clock cost are
recorded.

\paragraph{E --- Guidance and control comparison}
An infinite-horizon discrete LQR \cite{anderson1990} is compared with a
receding-horizon MPC \cite{mayne2000} using the LQR solution as its terminal
cost, following Breger and How~\cite{breger2008}. Both controllers act on the
same HCW plant from the same initial state with identical state and control
weights, sample time and thrust limit, and the approach-cone constraint is
disabled so that thrust saturation is the only active constraint and the
difference between them cannot be attributed to constraint feasibility.

\paragraph{F --- End-to-end evaluation}
The complete loop is exercised over a dispersed Monte Carlo campaign in which
the initial range, bearing, closing speed, tumble rate, target attitude and
navigation initialisation error are drawn per trial. Three configurations ---
perfect state knowledge, direct noisy measurements with the filter, and the
full vision pipeline --- are compared trial-by-trial on a shared seed
sequence, so the configuration is the only difference between paired trials. A
second study sweeps the pixel noise over the same paired dispersion to locate
the point at which the perception chain becomes limiting, and per-step
measurement availability is pooled by instantaneous range to identify the
mechanism.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

Translation errors are Euclidean norms of the estimate minus the truth.
Attitude error is the geodesic angle between the estimated and true
orientations,
\begin{equation}
    e_{R}
    =
    \cos^{-1}
    \left[
    \frac{\operatorname{tr}(\hat{\mathbf{R}}\mathbf{R}^{T})-1}{2}
    \right],
\end{equation}
and image-space consistency is measured by the mean reprojection error. For a
sequence of $N$ samples the reported RMSE is
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{RMSE}(e) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}e_k^2},
\end{equation}
navigation RMSE being this quantity applied to the position error of the
filtered state over a complete closed-loop run. Control effort is the total
velocity increment
\begin{equation}
    \Delta v = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\|\mathbf{u}_k\|_2\,\Delta t,
\end{equation}
and rendezvous accuracy is the terminal range $\|\mathbf{r}_f\|_2$. A trial
docks if it satisfies the range and closing-speed criterion of
Table~\ref{tab:experimental_parameters} within the simulation horizon;
docking success is the fraction of trials that do so. Computational cost is
reported as wall-clock time or as time per control step.
